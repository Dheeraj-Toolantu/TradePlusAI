VWAP-EMA Scalper

A 1-minute scalping strategy for NIFTY, BANKNIFTY and SENSEX options. It combines VWAP, the 5 and 9 EMAs, option delta, open interest, PCR, the premium's volume profile and India VIX. The premium is picked from the OI data. Risk-reward is set from delta and gamma, and trades are managed with a staged trailing stop.

1. The strategy at a glance

The VWAP-EMA Scalper buys an index option (a CE for a long, a PE for a short) when the 1-minute index tape and the option chain agree on a short, clean move. It enters only when all seven criteria pass, worth 10 points in total, and every V5 rule gate is green.

  1. Trend and location. Price is on the trade side of a sloping session VWAP and is not stretched away from it.
  2. Momentum trigger. The 5 EMA leads the 9 EMA, price pulls back to the 9 EMA, and a candle resumes the move on above-average futures volume.
  3. Option confirmation. The selected strike has a delta of 0.40-0.60. Option writers (OI) and the PCR support the side. The premium itself is accepted above its point of control. India VIX is in a tradeable range.
  4. Plan. The stop is placed on the index chart (structure), then translated to the premium with delta and gamma. The target is the chosen 1:1 or 1:2 multiple. A trade is refused if charges eat more than a quarter of the reward.
  5. Execution and management. The trade is placed on the paper Smart Scalper, automatically or with one click. The stop then moves to a cost-covered breakeven, locks profit, and trails.

3. Choosing the side

The side comes from where price sits relative to VWAP and how the two EMAs are stacked, on the latest completed candle:

ConditionSideBuys
Close > VWAP and 5 EMA > 9 EMALONGCE (call)
Close < VWAP and 5 EMA < 9 EMASHORTPE (put)
VWAP and EMAs disagreeLean only (the side of VWAP)Nothing until both agree
Close exactly on VWAPNoneNothing

Only option buying is used. The PE side is the exact mirror of the CE side throughout this document: above becomes below, rising becomes falling, highs become lows.

4. The seven criteria

Each criterion scores points. The strategy trades only at 10 / 10, so every criterion is effectively mandatory. The score is shown so a trader can see how close a setup is. The rules below are for a long (CE); the short (PE) side mirrors them.

① VWAP 2 points

  • 1 point: the close is above the session VWAP and not more than 1.5 × ATR(1m) away from it.
  • 1 point: VWAP has risen over the last 5 bars.
  • VWAP is weighted by near-month futures volume. Without volume it can't be trusted, so the criterion fails ("VWAP unobservable").

Why: VWAP is the session's fair price, where institutions benchmark fills. A long above a rising VWAP is with the session's flow. Stretching far above it is chasing, and those moves often snap back to VWAP inside a scalp's time frame.

② 5 / 9 EMA 2 points

  • 1 point, the stack: 5 EMA > 9 EMA > VWAP.
  • 1 point, the trigger: within the last 3 bars, a candle's low tagged the 9 EMA (tolerance 0.05 ATR). Then a candle that is green, closes above the 5 EMA and above the previous candle's high, on futures volume of at least 1.2× the prior 20-bar average.
  • Freshness: the trigger must be the latest completed candle, or the one before it if price hasn't run more than 0.5 ATR past the trigger close and is still above the 5 EMA.

Why: the 5/9 EMA pair is the classic fast momentum read on 1-minute charts. Buying the first resumption after a shallow pullback to the 9 EMA gives a defined stop just under the pullback. The volume check filters out drifts that nobody is trading.

③ Delta 1 point

  • The auto-selected strike's |delta| is between 0.40 and 0.60, near the money.

Why: a scalp needs a premium that moves with the index point for point. Below 0.40 (OTM) the premium barely reacts and theta decay dominates. Above 0.60 (deep ITM) the option is a costly proxy for the future, with less leverage per rupee.

④ Open interest 2 points

  • 1 point: the 5-minute OI writer-flow score is +1 or more. Put writing or call short covering is outweighing call writing.
  • 1 point: at the ATM strike there is supportive build-up: put short build-up (put writing), call short covering, or call long build-up. There must also be no fresh call writing at the ATM, and the largest call-OI wall above spot must be farther away than the target (in index points).

Why: option writers are the best-capitalised participants. Put writers defend support; call writers cap rallies. A long needs writers leaning its way and no big call wall sitting between the entry and the target.

⑤ PCR (put-call ratio, OI) 1 point

  • Long: PCR 0.80 to 1.60 and not falling over 5 minutes. Short: PCR 0.50 to 1.20 and not rising.
  • Outside 0.50-1.60 the PCR counts as stretched, which signals reversal risk, and the criterion fails on either side.

Why: a healthy, rising PCR means puts are being written under the market (support). Extreme readings mean positioning is crowded and tends to unwind.

⑥ Volume profile of the selected premium 1 point

  • Built from the selected premium's own 1-minute candles today: the point of control (POC) and the 70% value area (VAL to VAH).
  • Passes when the premium closes above its POC and either at or above the VAH, or on a volume burst of at least 1.5× the prior 20 bars.

Why: the option must confirm the index. A premium accepted above the price where most of today's contracts traded shows buyers in control of that contract, not just the underlying.

⑦ India VIX 1 point

  • VIX regime LOW, NORMAL or HIGH (not EXTREME) and a VIX value between 10 and 24.

Why: below about 10, premiums hardly move and charges dominate. In extreme volatility, 1-minute structure breaks down, spreads widen and stops get run.

5. Auto-selecting the premium from OI

The strategy picks the contract itself; the trader doesn't choose a strike. Candidates are the trade side's options (calls for a long) with:

  • |delta| between 0.35 and 0.65. This is a wider net than the 0.40-0.60 the delta criterion requires, so near-misses can be shown.
  • A premium of at least ₹20. Where a bid/ask is available, a spread of at most 3%; wider spreads are penalised.

Each candidate is ranked by a selection score:

score = 4 × (OI rank + volume rank) / (2 × (n − 1)) ← liquidity, 0-4 + 2 if the option's 5-minute activity is LONG_BUILDUP or SHORT_COVERING − 2 if it is SHORT_BUILDUP (fresh writing of that option) + 2 × (1 − min(|delta − 0.5| / 0.15, 1)) ← closeness to delta 0.50, 0-2 − 3 if the spread is wider than 3%

The top-ranked strike becomes the selected premium. On the Scalper desk the trade side's card and chart switch to it, marked with an OI pick badge. The ▲▼ buttons still override it until the next new pick. The next three candidates are listed with their scores.

6. The premium's volume profile

Each 1-minute premium candle's volume is spread evenly across the price bins its high-low range covers, using 24 bins over the day's range.

  • POC: the bin with the most volume, where most contracts changed hands.
  • Value area: grown outward from the POC, always adding the busier neighbouring bin, until it holds 70% of the volume. Its top is the VAH and its bottom the VAL.

The criterion asks whether the premium is accepted above value: a close above the POC, plus either a break of the VAH or a 1.5× volume burst. The chart draws the POC, VAH and VAL as dashed lines.

7. Stop, target and delta-gamma risk-reward

7.1 Structure first: the stop on the index

stop level = min(pullback low, 9 EMA) − 0.15 × ATR(1m) (long) R (points) = entry close − stop level if R < 0.30 × ATR → widen R to 0.30 × ATR (tighter is inside 1-minute noise) if R > 1.50 × ATR → NO TRADE (too wide to be a scalp) target level = entry + k × R, k = 1 (1:1) or k = 2 (1:2)

The stop is where the scalp is proven wrong: back through the pullback and the 9 EMA. The target is a multiple of that risk, so reward-to-risk is fixed by design, not hoped for.

7.2 Mapping index points to the premium with delta and gamma

An option premium does not move one-for-one with the index. For a small index move x, its change is approximately:

Δpremium(x) ≈ |delta| · x + ½ · gamma · x²

Applied to the stop and the target, with theta charged for the expected 10-minute hold:

theta drag = |theta per day| × 10 / 375 (375 trading minutes a day) premium SL = P − ( |delta| · R − ½ · gamma · R² + theta drag ) premium target = P + ( |delta| · kR + ½ · gamma · (kR)² − theta drag ) P = entry premium (the ask); delta from the chain; gamma from Black-Scholes using the chain's IV and expiry

Why gamma matters. Gamma makes the premium's response convex. On an adverse move the long option loses a little less than delta alone predicts (−½γR²). On a favourable move it gains a little more (+½γ(kR)²). The premium reward-to-risk is therefore usually slightly better than the index ratio. Theta and tick rounding take some of that back.

Effect by expiry. With a week or more to expiry, gamma is small and the correction is a few paise. On expiry day gamma is large, so premium stops and targets move fast. The panel flags expiry days.

Both prices are rounded to the contract's tick (₹0.05).

7.3 The reward-to-risk gate

The V5 R:R gate compares the premium reward-to-risk with the ratio the trader chose, allowing 3% for tick rounding. Every other strategy in TradePulse keeps its 2.0 minimum.

8. Charges and position size

Every plan is costed per lot, for both exits (target and stop), with a model tuned for liquid near-ATM index options:

ComponentRate
Brokerage₹20 per order (₹40 round trip)
STT0.1% of the sell premium
Exchange transaction charge0.03503% of premium turnover
SEBI fee / stamp duty₹10 per crore / 0.003% of the buy premium
GST18% on brokerage + exchange + SEBI
Slippage0.15% per side, beyond the spread
SpreadThe chain's bid/ask, capped at 0.5% of the premium per side (Groww's chain has no live bid/ask; the route estimates it from IV)
The 25% ruleIf round-trip charges at the target exceed 25% of the reward, the trade is refused: the move is too small to scalp profitably. In practice a 1:1 NIFTY scalp with a 10-15 point stop is usually refused (about a ₹6 premium move, roughly a third of it lost to charges). 1:2 is the practical default; 1:1 passes when the structural move is larger, for example on BANKNIFTY.

Lots. lots = ⌊ max loss per trade ÷ (premium risk × lot size + charges at the stop) ⌋, capped by the Scalper's lots setting. The defaults are a ₹1,500 max loss and 1 lot, taken from the Scalper desk's own controls. If one lot already risks more than the max loss, there is no trade.

Every placement takes the same safe path:

  1. Server re-check. POST /api/algo-trading with action: PLACE_ON_SCALPER re-runs the strategy and all V5 gates on the server. The browser can't confirm a trade.
  2. Book limits. The Smart Scalper applies its own limits: kill switch, SAFE_MODE, daily loss limit, a 5-minute cool-down after a loss, a stop after 3 consecutive losses, the trade cap, one engine position per index, and 09:15-15:15 IST on trading days only.
  3. Fill. The leg is filled at the live ask, plus the scalper's configured slippage. Slippage reduces the reward, never the stop distance.
Paper onlyAutomatic placement never sends a live order. Real-money orders remain on TradePulse's separate live path: /api/live-orders, with a preview, a PIN, the LIVE limits and the kill switch.

The trail stage, the initial SL and the charges per unit are saved with the order, so a server restart resumes the same trail. On the premium chart the risk-reward drawing follows the live SL. Once the SL is above the entry, it shows the profit locked, e.g. "SL · +0.5R locked ₹124.32 (+₹225.55)". Keep the execution page open: exits are evaluated by the Scalper desk's 10-second scan, which runs in the browser while the page is open, the same way the Smart Scalper has always worked.

15. Limitations and first-live checklist

  • Not yet run on live data. The build environment could not reach market data. The strategy is covered by tests on synthetic tapes (21 Python and 14 TypeScript tests for the strategy, placement, trail and chart), and the full suites pass. Watch the first live sessions in paper mode.
  • Check on the first live sessions:
    • near-month futures volume arrives on the 1-minute index candles, so VWAP is observable;
    • market-intel's per-strike 5-minute OI change and build-up are populated (they need a 5-minute baseline after the open);
    • the selected premium's 1-minute candles load for the volume profile;
    • the chain's delta and IV look sane on expiry day.
  • Spread estimate. Groww's chain carries no live bid/ask, so the spread is estimated from IV and capped. Real fills may differ.
  • 1:1 rarely qualifies on NIFTY because of the 25% charges rule. That is by design, not a fault.
  • Browser-driven management. Exits run while the execution page is open.
  • Paper first. Run the strategy in paper mode for several weeks and review the closed-trade log (exit reasons, R multiples, charges) before considering live trading through TradePulse's PIN-confirmed flow.
