NIFTY OPTIONS INTELLIGENT RECOMMENDATION ENGINE
==================================================
COMPLETE IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
Version: 1.0

PURPOSE
-------
Build a deterministic, explainable, configurable and auditable NIFTY Options
Recommendation Engine for CALL and PUT candidates.

The engine must identify the strongest risk-adjusted setup from available
market data. It must NOT claim that any option guarantees profit or produces
"maximum profit". Recommendations must be analytical signals based on the
configured scoring, feasibility and risk framework.

==================================================
TASK 1 — RECOMMENDATION PIPELINE
==================================================

Implement this complete pipeline:

LIVE MARKET DATA
      |
      v
NIFTY MARKET DIRECTION
      |
      v
TREND CONFIRMATION
      |
      v
OPTION CHAIN ANALYSIS
      |
      v
OPTION CANDIDATE FILTERING
      |
      v
7-FACTOR SCORING ENGINE
      |
      v
MOVE FEASIBILITY CHECK
      |
      v
RISK/REWARD VALIDATION
      |
      v
CONFIDENCE CALCULATION
      |
      v
OPTION RANKING
      |
      v
RECOMMENDED CONTRACT
      |
      v
EXPLANATION + WARNINGS

The engine must evaluate multiple strikes and must not automatically select
only the nearest ATM option.

==================================================
TASK 2 — DIRECTIONAL SCORE
==================================================

Create a normalized Directional Score from 0–100.

Evaluate, where data is available:
- Trend direction
- Price action
- EMA alignment
- VWAP relationship
- RSI
- MACD
- ADX
- Market structure
- Support/resistance
- Higher-timeframe confirmation

Determine:
- BULLISH
- BEARISH
- NEUTRAL

Example:
Direction = BULLISH
Directional Score = 82

For CALL candidates:
- Bullish direction = positive
- Neutral direction = reduced
- Strong bearish direction = reject or heavily penalize

For PUT candidates:
- Bearish direction = positive
- Neutral direction = reduced
- Strong bullish direction = reject or heavily penalize

If the market is neutral or conflicting, the engine should be able to return
NO TRADE / WAIT instead of forcing a CALL or PUT.

The engine must store the reasons supporting the directional score.

Example structure:
{
  "direction": "BULLISH",
  "directional_score": 82,
  "reason": [
    "Price above VWAP",
    "20 EMA above 50 EMA",
    "Higher timeframe trend bullish"
  ]
}

==================================================
TASK 3 — SEVEN-FACTOR SCORING MODEL
==================================================

Use the following default weights:

Directional Score       25%
Trend Confirmation      20%
Delta Quality            15%
IV / IV Rank             10%
OI / Change in OI        10%
Volume / Liquidity       10%
Risk / Reward             10%
--------------------------------
TOTAL                    100%

Every factor must produce a normalized score from 0–100.

Final Score:

Final Score =
    Directional Score  * 0.25
  + Trend Score        * 0.20
  + Delta Score        * 0.15
  + IV Score           * 0.10
  + OI Score           * 0.10
  + Liquidity Score    * 0.10
  + RiskReward Score   * 0.10

The final score must remain between 0 and 100.

Missing data must not silently be treated as a valid score. Apply a defined
missing-data policy and generate a data-quality warning.

==================================================
TASK 4 — TREND CONFIRMATION (20%)
==================================================

Create a multi-timeframe trend engine.

Minimum timeframes:
- 5m
- 15m
- 30m
- 1H

Determine trend for each timeframe:
- BULLISH
- BEARISH
- NEUTRAL

Example:
5m  = BULLISH
15m = BULLISH
30m = BULLISH
1H  = NEUTRAL

Detect:
- Trend alignment
- Trend conflict
- Trend reversal
- Range-bound market
- Breakout
- Breakdown

Higher-timeframe confirmation should have greater importance than very
short-term noise.

Generate a normalized Trend Confirmation Score from 0–100.

==================================================
TASK 5 — DELTA QUALITY (15%)
==================================================

Do not treat higher delta as automatically better.

Evaluate delta relative to the selected strategy profile.

Default preferred ranges for directional long options:

0.45–0.65 = preferred
0.30–0.45 = acceptable
0.20–0.30 = aggressive
<0.20       = high risk / reject by default

For PUTs use absolute delta values.

These ranges must be configurable.

Example:
{
  "delta": 0.54,
  "delta_score": 92,
  "delta_category": "ATM_BALANCED"
}

==================================================
TASK 6 — IV / IV RANK (10%)
==================================================

Evaluate:
- Current IV
- IV Rank
- IV Percentile
- Historical IV
- Relative IV versus nearby strikes

Do not assume low IV is always better or high IV is always bad.

For long options:
- Extremely high IV can be penalized
- Moderate IV may be favorable
- Low IV may be favorable
- Event risk and expected IV changes must be considered where data exists

Example:
{
  "iv": 10.7,
  "iv_rank": 28,
  "iv_score": 84
}

If IV Rank or another input is unavailable, do not fabricate it.

==================================================
TASK 7 — OI / CHANGE IN OI (10%)
==================================================

Analyze:
- Call OI
- Put OI
- Change in Call OI
- Change in Put OI
- OI concentration
- Nearby-strike OI
- OI-derived support/resistance
- PCR

Detect, where the data supports the classification:
- Call writing
- Call unwinding
- Put writing
- Put unwinding
- OI buildup
- Short covering
- Long buildup
- Long unwinding

For CALL candidates, assess whether option-chain structure supports upside.
For PUT candidates, assess whether option-chain structure supports downside.

Do not use PCR alone to generate a recommendation.

==================================================
TASK 8 — VOLUME / LIQUIDITY (10%)
==================================================

Evaluate:
- Volume
- Average volume
- Bid price
- Ask price
- Bid-ask spread
- Open Interest
- OI change
- Market depth if available
- Quote freshness

Calculate:

Spread % =
(Ask - Bid) / Mid Price * 100

Penalize:
- Low volume
- Low OI
- Large bid/ask spread
- Poor liquidity
- Stale quotes

Prefer executable contracts over theoretically attractive but illiquid
contracts.

==================================================
TASK 9 — RISK / REWARD (10%)
==================================================

For every candidate calculate:
- Entry Premium
- Stop Loss
- Target
- Maximum Premium Risk
- Potential Reward
- Risk/Reward Ratio
- Breakeven

For a long CALL:
Expiry Breakeven = Strike + Premium

For a long PUT:
Expiry Breakeven = Strike - Premium

Where applicable, include:
- Brokerage
- Exchange charges
- Taxes
- Slippage

Do not display an R:R merely because a configured value says 2.0.
Calculate it from the actual entry, stop and target.

Example:
Entry = 112.40
Stop = 84.00
Target = 169.20

Risk   = Entry - Stop
Reward = Target - Entry
R:R    = Reward / Risk

==================================================
TASK 10 — MOVE FEASIBILITY CHECK
==================================================

This is a mandatory component.

Do not select an option purely because it has the highest score.

For every candidate perform:

Expected NIFTY Move
        |
        v
Required Move to Breakeven
        |
        v
Required Move to Target
        |
        v
Option Sensitivity
        |
        v
Probability / Confidence Estimate
        |
        v
Feasibility

The feasibility result must influence the final candidate selection.

==================================================
TASK 11 — EXPECTED NIFTY MOVE
==================================================

Estimate the expected underlying move using available market data.

Possible inputs:
- ATR
- Historical volatility
- Implied volatility
- Option-derived expected move
- Recent trading range
- Support/resistance
- Current trend
- Time remaining to expiry

Do not blindly combine all methods.

Create a configurable methodology, for example:
- ATR-based expected move
- IV-based expected move
- Realized-volatility expected move

These may be combined using configurable weights.

Return:
{
  "expected_move_points": 180,
  "expected_range": {
    "lower": 23100,
    "upper": 23460
  }
}

The methodology and weights must be configurable.

==================================================
TASK 12 — BREAKEVEN FEASIBILITY
==================================================

For every option calculate the required NIFTY movement to reach expiry
breakeven.

CALL:
Breakeven = Strike + Premium
Required Move = Breakeven - Current NIFTY

PUT:
Breakeven = Strike - Premium
Required Move = Current NIFTY - Breakeven

Required Move % =
Required Move / NIFTY Spot * 100

Compare:
Required Move
versus
Expected Move

Create a Breakeven Feasibility Score.

Example:
Required Move = 142 points
Expected Move = 180 points
Feasibility = FAVORABLE

If:
Required Move > Expected Move

apply a meaningful penalty.

==================================================
TASK 13 — TARGET FEASIBILITY
==================================================

Estimate what underlying NIFTY movement may be required for the option to
reach the configured premium target.

Use, where available:
- Delta
- Gamma
- Theta
- IV
- Time to expiry
- Underlying movement

Do not simply assume that an option premium will rise by a fixed percentage
without checking underlying sensitivity.

Clearly label target calculations as ESTIMATED.

Targets are not guaranteed.

==================================================
TASK 14 — CONFIDENCE MODEL
==================================================

Maintain separate concepts:

SETUP SCORE
CONFIDENCE

Example:
Setup Score: 91/100
Confidence: HIGH

Never convert:
91 score = 91% probability

That conversion is invalid unless a statistically validated probability model
has been developed and calibrated.

Confidence should depend on:
- Directional alignment
- Trend alignment
- Move feasibility
- Liquidity
- Option-chain confirmation
- Risk/reward
- Data quality

If a validated statistical probability model exists, show the methodology,
sample size, calibration and limitations.

==================================================
TASK 15 — CANDIDATE FILTERING
==================================================

Before scoring, reject or heavily penalize unsuitable candidates.

Reject by default when:
- Option is illiquid
- Bid/ask spread exceeds configured limit
- OI is below minimum
- Volume is below minimum
- Data is stale
- Required move is unrealistic
- Delta is outside allowed range
- R:R is below minimum
- Market direction strongly conflicts with option direction

Example configuration:
{
  "minimum_rr": 1.5,
  "maximum_spread_percent": 2.0,
  "minimum_oi": 10000,
  "minimum_volume": 5000,
  "minimum_delta": 0.20
}

All thresholds must be configurable.

==================================================
TASK 16 — STRIKE SELECTION
==================================================

Evaluate multiple strikes around ATM.

Default candidate window:
- ATM - 3 strikes
- ATM - 2 strikes
- ATM - 1 strike
- ATM
- ATM + 1 strike
- ATM + 2 strikes
- ATM + 3 strikes

Evaluate:
- ITM
- ATM
- OTM

Do not automatically select:
- Cheapest premium
- Highest delta
- Highest raw score
- Nearest ATM

unless the selected strategy profile explicitly requires it.

Selection must result from the complete scoring + feasibility + risk process.

==================================================
TASK 17 — FINAL RECOMMENDATION LOGIC
==================================================

The final decision must consider:

Base Score
+
Move Feasibility
+
Risk/Reward
+
Liquidity
+
Data Quality

Select based on risk-adjusted suitability rather than maximum theoretical
profit.

Support configurable strategy profiles:

- BALANCED
- AGGRESSIVE
- CONSERVATIVE
- HIGH_MOMENTUM
- INTRADAY
- SWING

Each profile may modify:
- Factor weights
- Delta preference
- Minimum R:R
- Maximum spread
- Move-feasibility threshold
- Candidate range
- Risk limits

Weights must always sum to 100%.

TASK 27 — MATHEMATICAL SAFETY RULES
==================================================

Never:

1. Convert score directly into probability.
2. Assume higher delta automatically means better.
3. Assume lower premium automatically means better.
4. Assume high IV automatically means bad.
5. Assume high PCR automatically means bullish.
6. Assume high OI automatically means support/resistance.
7. Select the cheapest option merely because percentage return can be higher.
8. Guarantee profit.
9. Guarantee target achievement.
10. Fabricate missing market data.
11. Use future information in historical backtests.
12. Ignore transaction costs and slippage when evaluating backtest results.

FINAL PRINCIPLE
==================================================

The system must follow this decision hierarchy:

MARKET DIRECTION
      +
TREND CONFIRMATION
      +
OPTION QUALITY
      +
LIQUIDITY
      +
RISK/REWARD
      +
EXPECTED MOVE
      +
BREAKEVEN FEASIBILITY
      +
TARGET FEASIBILITY
      +
DATA QUALITY
      |
      v
RISK-ADJUSTED OPTION SELECTION

The engine must prefer a well-supported, feasible and executable setup over
an option that merely has a cheap premium or attractive theoretical percentage
return.

The engine must be allowed to say:

NO TRADE / WAIT

when the evidence is insufficient.

The recommendation is an analytical model output, not a guarantee of profit.
